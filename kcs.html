CFD case study · Hydrodynamics

KCS resistance prediction & validation.

A numerical investigation of the KRISO Container Ship, built as a reliable foundation for later CFD–DEM ship–ice simulations.

STAR-CCM+RANSk–ω SSTVOF

The objective

Establish a credible resistance workflow before introducing the complexity of fragmented ice.

The KCS was selected because extensive experimental data exists for comparison. The work focused on the computational domain, mesh strategy, solver settings and repeatable post-processing required to predict total resistance with confidence.

01 — Computational setup

A model-scale container ship in a controlled numerical ocean.

Domain & boundaries

A three-dimensional open-water domain follows ITTC practice. The hull remains fixed while water and air enter at the target velocity, recreating the relative motion of a ship advancing through calm water.

The full hull is retained, rather than using a symmetry plane, so the setup can later accommodate asymmetric ice-floe fields.

02 — Mesh & numerics

Resolution where the flow matters most.

Mesh strategy

  • Trimmed-cell mesh with local refinement zones
  • Refined free surface to capture waves accurately
  • Prism layers along the hull to resolve the near-wall flow
  • Wall-function approach targeting y+ around 30

Solver strategy

Unsteady RANS is coupled with the k–ω SST turbulence model. The Volume of Fluid method tracks the air–water interface; five inner iterations and a 0.01 s time step provided stable convergence.

03 — Validation

Comparing resistance with experimental reference data.

The approach was assessed across Froude numbers from 0.06 to 0.18. Once the clean-water case was established, the same workflow was extended to ice concentrations of 60%, 70% and 80%.

High-speed agreement
At 60% and 70% concentration, total resistance predictions were generally within about 10% at the higher test speeds.
Convergence
Time-averaging the contact forces over approximately 40 seconds produced a stable mean ice-resistance value.
Useful limitations
Low-speed and dense-ice results reveal where the floe physics, rather than the base hydrodynamic workflow, needs improvement.

What I learned

Validation is not a final checkbox: it is the engineering framework that makes a more complex simulation trustworthy.

Build from the physics

Mesh, free-surface resolution and near-wall treatment directly shape the resistance prediction.

Measure convergence

Transient contact forces require a carefully chosen averaging interval, not a single instantaneous value.

Make limitations explicit

Identifying premature floe rotation gives a concrete direction for the next model iteration.
