Added resistance of a ship navigating in ice floes.

A CFD–DEM study of how a vessel, free-surface flow and fragmented sea ice interact — built to make resistance prediction more practical for design and research.

Simcenter STAR-CCM+CFD–DEMKCS benchmark

The question

Can simulation make ship–ice resistance studies more repeatable, flexible and useful?

As Arctic and Antarctic waters become more accessible, ships increasingly encounter fragmented ice rather than continuous sheets. Towing-tank experiments remain essential, but they are costly and every random ice field is different. This thesis develops a controlled virtual test environment that resolves water, hull and individual ice floes together.

01 — Method

One model, three interacting systems.

01

Water & air

RANS flow equations with a k–ω SST turbulence model describe the surrounding flow. The Volume of Fluid method captures the moving free surface.

02

Ship & motion

The KRISO Container Ship provides a well-documented reference hull, enabling direct comparison with published experiments.

03

Ice floes

Each floe is a discrete DEM particle. Hull–floe and floe–floe contacts are resolved as the field rearranges around the vessel.

02 — Development focus

Ice-field generation

Algorithms generate prescribed concentrations and realistic floe-size distributions, including fields up to 80% concentration.

Contact calibration

DEM parameters reproduce how floes collide, slide and transmit forces through the field.

Hydrodynamic correction

Corrections address added-mass moment and drag torque that influence particle rotation.

Automated analysis

Python tools support case preparation, post-processing and experiment comparisons.

03 — Validation

Measured against the KCS benchmark.

The setup was evaluated from Fr = 0.06 to 0.18, at 60%, 70% and 80% ice concentration. The primary metric is total resistance, compared with towing-tank data.

60% / 70%
At higher speeds, added resistance is predicted with errors close to or below 10%.
80%
Dense floe fields expose the importance of geometry and force chains.
Low speed
Premature floe rotation remains the key source of under-prediction.

The takeaway

CFD–DEM is a reliable tool for investigating added resistance in fragmented ice — while making its remaining physics limitations visible and measurable.

04 — Next steps

From circular particles to more faithful ice physics.

Particle clumps

Represent each floe with multiple elements for more faithful geometry and hydrodynamics.

Realistic shapes

Explore polygonal floes and near-complete concentrations.

Two-way coupling

Study feedback from dense ice fields to flow and ship waves.

Operational motion

Extend the approach to self-propulsion and manoeuvring.
